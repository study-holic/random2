## Prompt A (DeepSeek: rules-heavy)

You are advising a solo team in Cambridge Battlecode 2026, a programming game where each team writes a bot (C++ compiled to WebAssembly). We need a critical second opinion, not reassurance.

**The game.**
- Two teams each draft champions from 11 (Ryth, Vesper, Vendetta, Cassi & Issac (one pick, two bodies), Stellar, Prism, Marcella, Willow, Ren, Varg, Tavi). Team size is 3, 4 or 5 picks depending on the map, on 17 fixed maps.
- Win: tribute 1,000 gems first. Otherwise the team with more tributed gems at round 2,000 wins; ties go to lifetime gold earned, then kills, then Team B.
- Camps: wolves, Elks, gem clusters, gold clusters and monoliths (20,000 health, 200 Strength a hit, 200 gems, 300 gold, 150-round respawn). Kill every monster in a cardinally connected group, then stand on one of its spawn tiles uncontested for 5 rounds to capture its gems and gold. Any enemy on the region resets the timer, and any occupied spawn tile delays the respawn.
- Tribute at an altar is a channel of max(1, ceil(gems/10)) of the owner's turns. Damage does not cancel it; a stun, death, or a new altar lock does. A lock costs 10 gold, is placed within 2 tiles, blocks the opponents' tributes there, and takes 3 basic attacks to clear.
- Carried gems pass to the killer on death; a kill also pays 50 gold, and the victim is out for 60 rounds.
- Each champion runs its own process with 100,000 fuel a turn (fuel = metered instructions).

**Facts (18 October 2026, our measurements unless marked).**
1. The Sprint 1 tournament is single elimination, 15 games a match, open to all eligible teams, and seeded from the ladder. The qualification snapshot is at 23:59 BST tonight. We have 0 submissions and a 1,500 rating.
2. Local test panel: six bots (an older version of our own bot, another team's public bot "foundry-v16", and four public clones of top teams), each played on 17 maps from both sides, 204 games. A full panel run takes about 2 minutes on 13 cores.
3. Games are deterministic: a rerun is identical. But behaviour depends on fuel left, so a change that does nothing except spend about 15,000 extra fuel on turn 1 flipped 15 of 204 results (+9 −6). On one map, a one-sample comparison scored that no-op change +3 of 12 games.
4. Panel wins out of 204 for four local versions: v4.14: 143. v4.15: 147, with 4 flips to wins and 0 to losses against v4.14 (3 of the 4 on the map where the no-op also scored +3). v418d: 152 (17 flips to wins, 8 to losses; better on 5 maps, worse against the two strongest panel bots on 4). microstella-next: 147. None passes more than 10 of 17 maps on "at least 7 of 12 games and 2 of the 4 games against the two strongest bots". A coin-flip bot would pass a map 34.9 % of the time.

Ladder data (9,245 ranked games, 18 Oct): champion win excess after rating adjustment, |z| ≥ 3: stampede Prism +11.3 points (n 312), ambush Prism −25.0 (n 88), rows Willow +18.6 (n 84), rows Marcella −27.5 (n 49), fragment Varg −8.1 (n 436). 6. On one map ("swirl", 45×30) our bot dies 45 to 50 times a game, including 43.5 a game against the do-nothing starter bot, and still wins 7 of 12 panel games.
7. Six of the 17 local map files are older layouts of maps that were changed on 16 October; the current files need a sign-in we have not done yet.
8. In earlier tests against panel bots, chipping a monolith lost 6 of 6 games. The opponent took the capture after our champions wore it down.

## Prompt B (Copilot: code and data)

You are reviewing the release process for a C++ game bot in a 1-hour-30-minute sprint. Be critical; we want the failure points.

**Setup.** Cambridge Battlecode 2026. Each team's bot is C++ compiled to WebAssembly, and each unit is a process with a 100,000-instruction ("fuel") budget a turn. Two teams draft 3 to 5 champions per map on 17 maps. The race is to bank 1,000 gems at altars. Otherwise the lead at round 2,000 wins, with lifetime gold as the tiebreak. Gems come from clearing monster camps and standing on the region for 5 rounds. Tribute takes ceil(gems/10) turns and is cancelled by a stun, a death or an enemy "altar lock" (10 gold, 3 hits to clear). Tonight there is one upload before a single-elimination tournament snapshot at 23:59 BST.

**Harness (our code).**
- `mapgate.py`: our bot against 6 panel bots × 17 maps × 2 sides = 204 games through `cambc run-batch --jobs 13`, which takes about 107 s. It parses per-game JSON (outcome, rounds, submitted gems, kills and deaths from events, earned gold, bot faults).
- Games are deterministic, so a rerun is identical. They are also fuel-chaotic: adding a single extra `camp_groups()` call on turn 1 (about 15,000 fuel) flipped 15 of 204 results (+9 −6), concentrated on 5 maps, while 10 maps stayed identical.
- To get noise samples, we build "salted" copies that burn s × 4,000 loop iterations of fuel on turn 1 (s = 1, 2), and run 3 salts per bot (612 games).
- `cmpcells.py`: per (map, opponent, side) cell, the win rate and gem margin averaged over salts. Per map, it admits a change when ΔW ≥ 1.5 games of 12, the summed gem-margin change is > 0, and there is no fall against the 2 strongest panel bots.

**Data.**
- Panel wins of 204: v4.14 143; v4.15 147 (+4 −0 flips vs v4.14, 3 of them on the map where the no-op also scored +3); v418d 152 (+17 −8; better on pilgrimage +4, interlock +2, lanes +2, collision +1 and eight +1; worse against the strong pair on contest, elkring, fragment and stampede); microstella-next 147.
- Against the starter bot: v4.14 34/34, 0 faults. On one map ("swirl") it dies 43.5 times a game even against the starter bot.
- Ladder (9,245 games): rating-adjusted champion win excess with |z| ≥ 3 on 5 champion-map cells (for example, Prism +11.3 points on stampede, n 312; Prism −25.0 on ambush, n 88). 16 of 177 cells have |z| ≥ 2 against about 8 expected by chance.
- Six of the 17 local map files are pre-16-October layouts.

**Planned builds (in parallel, 4 jobs each).**
1. Per-map draft lists through a hook (`MapPlan::draft`).
2. A swirl and pilgrimage death-loop fix in per-map files.
3. A dispatcher: two complete bots (v4.15 and v418d) compiled into one WASM, each wrapped in its own namespace, with `main()` routing draft and play per map from a map fingerprint (dimensions and camp counts).

**Release tests.**
- R1: 34/34 against the starter bot, 0 faults.
- R2: no turn over 100,000 fuel.
- R3: mean panel wins over 3 salts ≥ the base's, no map that passed the 7/12 gate now fails it, and every changed map admitted by `cmpcells`.
- R4: a held-out panel of 5 other bots, 170 games, total ≥ base − 2.
- R5: a reviewer's verdict and one watched loss.

**The question.** Is this release process sound, and what is the best use of the remaining time? Options:
- (a) Ship the best existing version unchanged after R1 and R2.
- (b) Run the three builds and the R1 to R5 pipeline as described.
- (c) Drop one or more builds (which?) and spend the time on more salts or on the held-out panel.
- (d) Build a tactic instead (an altar-lock interrupt or a capture-denial step).
- (e) An option not listed.
- (f) None of these.

**Requests.**
1. Rank the options with reasons.
2. Name the failure mode of each, including code-level risks: namespace wrapping of two bots (static state, headers, symbol clashes, exceptions), fuel of the map fingerprint, and whether fuel-burn salts sample the noise that real code changes cause.
3. Is the admission rule's threshold (ΔW ≥ 1.5 of 12 with 3 salts) too loose or too strict? What would you use, and why?
4. Propose one idea from another game or competition that maps onto one of these mechanics, and name the mechanic.
5. Say which data point, if wrong, would change your answer.
